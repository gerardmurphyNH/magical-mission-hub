How to Write Your Tooth Fairy Letter

Two choices make the letter feel true: the quality inside the tooth, and what she did with its magic.

1 Choose your child's quality

Think of a real moment when you noticed this quality beginning to grow. The Tooth Fairy notices the small moments adults sometimes overlook.

Courage

Trying something new, speaking up, sleeping alone, joining a team, going to the dentist

Kindness

Helping a sibling, including someone, comforting a friend, sharing without being asked

Patience

Practicing something difficult, waiting calmly, sticking with a challenge, learning a new skill

Creativity

Drawing, building, inventing stories, making music, solving problems in a new way

Curiosity

Asking thoughtful questions, exploring how things work, noticing details adults miss

Persistence

Continuing after frustration, trying again after mistakes, finishing something hard

Wonder

Noticing nature, stars, animals, clouds, tiny details, or seeing magic in ordinary things

Friendship

Making someone feel included, listening carefully, forgiving someone, being loyal

Confidence

Trying something independently, performing, raising a hand, sharing an idea

Helpfulness

Pitching in at home, cleaning up, helping a younger child, offering to solve a problem

Imagination

Pretending, storytelling, building worlds, asking "what if?" questions

Honesty

Telling the truth, owning a mistake, being trusted with something important

2 Choose what she did with the magic

The Tooth Fairy works in quiet ways. She doesn't need giant miracles. Choose something connected to your child's world.

Animals your child loves

  • Loves owls: helped a baby owl safely find its nest
  • Loves dogs: helped a lost puppy hear its owner calling
  • Loves turtles: helped a tiny turtle reach the water
  • Loves butterflies: helped one find flowers after a storm
  • Loves dolphins: guided them through dark water
  • Loves rabbits: helped one find a safe hiding place

Places your child knows

  • A playground light flickering back on
  • A garden getting just enough rain
  • A squirrel finding its way across the yard
  • A bird finding a safe branch in the wind
  • A neighbour's flower blooming at just the right time
  • A lost mitten noticed before it disappeared forever

3 Or match the magic to the quality

CourageTonight, I used a little bit of that courage to help a nervous fox kit step out of its den and find its family.
KindnessTonight, I used a little bit of that kindness to help a lonely child feel brave enough to ask someone to play.
PatienceTonight, I used a little bit of that patience to calm the wind just long enough for a small bird to land safely.
CreativityTonight, I used a little bit of that creativity to help someone solve a problem they had almost given up on.
WonderTonight, I used a little bit of that wonder to make the stars shine a little brighter for someone who needed to look up.
One tip: name a real moment. "You were brave when you went first at swimming" lands far deeper than "you are brave."
